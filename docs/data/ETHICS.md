# ETHICS.md — The foundation of this map

**Cybersecurity Framework** catalogs tools and concepts so that defenders and learners of
all levels can find, understand, and use the right instruments — on systems **they own** or
hold **explicit written authorization** to assess.

A map is not a weapon. A map that lists "how to find a lock" is how locksmiths, students,
and homeowners describe their job — it is not an instruction manual for burglars. What
makes the difference is **intent and scope**, and both are encoded here *by design*.

## The four rules

1. **Authorized targets only.** Use every tool here exclusively on systems you own, or for
   which you hold written permission with defined scope and limits. This is the line
   between education and crime. Unauthorized access is prohibited and may violate the
   **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (18 U.S.C. §1030)**, the **Wiretap Act (18 U.S.C. §2511)**,
   **EU Directive 2013/40/EU**, and local cybercrime law.

2. **Teach and defend, don't enable.** Categories, tool picks, and "when to use" notes are
   written from the perspective of a tester/defender improving their own estate. The map's
   editorial policy is: **never a how-to break into a third party; always a how-to check
   your own.**

3. **Safety by default.** Tools are rated by effort, but more importantly this map surfaces
   the *staged* path: start in a local lab (VM, your own router, a disposable API key),
   learn the protocol, then — if ever — move to authorized real scope.

4. **Responsible disclosure.** If a tool or technique in this map leads you to a real bug on
   a real owner's system: report privately, allow a fix window, publish only after
   remediation. Never weaponize.

## Editorial standards for the tree

- Every category lists the **best-in-class tool** (★) and honest alternatives with *when to
  use each* — no hype, no marketing.
- Tools are marked by **effort** (beginner/medium/advanced) so newcomers can pick a sane
  entry path.
- Nothing here targets third parties by default; dry-run/offline defaults are preferred and
  called out.

## AS-IS

All listings are provided **AS IS**. The maintainers assume no liability for misuse. You are
solely responsible for your actions — and this map exists so that those actions be
educational, authorized, and defensible.
